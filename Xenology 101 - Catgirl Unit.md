chapter 3: The Nights Alone.

I didn’t understand what was happening, but the feelings were still etched into my heart - carved into my ribcage, jagged glass scraping my lungs. Before I tell you about the two weeks I still can’t remember, I need to tell you about one morning I do remember — the first time Martha tried to feed me, and why that mattered so much. Martha was my foster mother, and every year I write a letter to her describing my year, and thanking her for what she did for me. But I don't provide a way to call me back. I hope she understands why I write to her with pride about my daughters, and also why I am afraid of what I might do if I ever saw her in the same room as them. Its not rational to love and hate the same person so very much, but when is either of those emotions rational?

I must have slept alone some night before. Surely, there was a day when Mother and 'Mommy' went on a date and let me fall asleep alone, while my sister played. But there's being half asleep while your family talks in the room below, and then there's being alone in a much too soft, much too big, bed. When instead of the constant quiet purrs of the dream rattlers, reminding me of when I rested against Mommy's chest as a babe, there's just the desolate noise of the wind rattling the windows.

I remember feeling way too hot, even as sleet crashed against the window, my fur sticky with my sweat. But my arms kept pulling more and more clothes and blankets onto the bed, and then eventually under it. I didn't need softness under me, I needed to hide. To save my self until my mother could rescue me. They had told me that "I was safe" and then they had told me "Your uncle will never find you." to me, that was somewhere between a contradiction and a horrible rebuke. At the time I was mostly just terrified and confused.

The next morning, my "new mother", Martha Linton, opened the door and looked at the bed and frowned. I could clearly see her confusion from my hiding place under the bed. Looking back, I am pretty sure she was thinking that the lump on the bed was too big to be the small girl she had let into her home, and she wasn't wrong. I blinked and the shadows and sunbeams twitched into new patterns, my exhausted body simply skipping right from awake to awake again, the sleep in-between vanishing like mist. What had woken me was the smell of something very strange and very delicious. I hadn't ever smelled anything like it. I still can't smell teriyaki without feeling strange. Even if I do make it for my daughters, its just too tasty to not share with the girls I love so much.

But that morning all I knew was I was in a strange place, exhausted, and needed strong, filling, food. I snuck out of my room, and down the too noisy carpeted stairs, to find Martha, my foster mother cooking while my foster sisters ate, sitting on the kitchen table. My 'new sisters' were quiet, twitchy, flinching at noise, but they also were clearly pushing them selves to feel okay. That helped more then anything Martha did directly. Somehow, even in children of a different species, I could tell that while they feared the world, that at least, wanted, to trust Martha.

So I snunk across the waxy hardwood floor and onto the kitchen linoleum. I hooped up on to the bar stool, clawing at it's coarse textured pleather. Someone had told Martha and my sisters to not look directly at me. Which I will be forever grateful for, I still don't know who, quite possibly someone I still hate, but well many things about that time are complicated, contradictory. Like my feelings for Martha, as they they often are for foster parents. Or at least the good ones. She's a very good person, and I love her, and .. and I still kind of can't stand being around her, it just makes me feel so anxious so weak. But that's looking back. At the time I ... I needed her too much to love her as a person, or even as a parent. I was consumed by a need to do what she needed me to do. So I sat absolutely still at my place. They had somehow gotten the nameplate from my cubbie at school, so I was pretty sure that the food on the plate was meant for me. But I was too scared to eat it without direct permission. Still not looking directly at me. (Thank goodness), Martha said loudly, gently but loudly, "Lyra, you can start eating. Everything on your plate is for you." The lemon juice, the soy sauce, the pineapple, _the pineapple_. Mother, my real mother, cooked with a lot of fruit and loved pinaple, and was clearly _so proud_ that she could. My young self didn't understand the why, but children don't have to understand the why, to understand love, to me pineapple was .. was a symbol of my mothers love. I downed every bite of that stir-fry, even those weird bread-stick things I normally cant stand because they don't _taste_ like anything.

I still can remember mostly just sensory impressions. Questions that didn't make sense. Interrogators that were trying so hard to be kind, but not letting me just leave. Me begging to see my sister, full of guilt full of need to make sure she was okay. Everything scared me. I would beg to be allowed to stop talking, and then when they got up to get a glass of water, when they came back I would be standing so close to the door I would get hit as they opened it.

It got better after a while once they had the story they wanted. I had learned to not tell adult humans anything they didn't want to hear. I nearly passed out, when, years later, in college when I had to tell my professor I would be missing next class because I needed to go to a wedding.

I actually made some of my closest human friends of my life in the next few months. Shared trauma builds bonds. Most of them turned their lives around, escaped the cruel statistics of those that fall behind in school. And eventually as I got closer to those terrified hurt children, I grew to understand exactly what the people who put me here had _thought_ was happening. The pain, the horror, the neglect. Though I like many of you humans who interact with these victims, think that wider society obsesses too much about .. an almost mystical sense of purity, over just.. treating children like people, and not prizes, or objects to slate lusts on, sexual or otherwise.

Still I don't feel it is my place to complain on their behalf. I firmly believe in listening to the victims _first_, _last_, and _always_. Though also remembering that monsters can be exploited, recognizing the horrors that moral panics can perpetrate, and listening to _evidence_ not testimony, and not pseudoscience.

They finally let me see my sister. After the longest two weeks of my life, I was more scared then than at any point of my life before or sense. Not when the police came for us. Not when I defended my Phd thesis. Not when my eldest daughter was sent into combat. Not even when my mother died.

Those two weeks from when they told me "Your be allowed to see her soon." to when they finally actually let me see her. Those were the worst two weeks of my life. I don't even remember them, not really. Just that bone deep certainty that was the worst days of my life. I.. am not being vague because it hurts to talk about. It does. But that's not why. I literally don't remember a single thing that happened during those two weeks. You would think that was a blessing, and maybe it is. But I can still feel the numb fear, my instincts yelling at me that I should just hide as deeply as I possibly could and wait to die.

Finally I got to see her. She was showing then. What should have been, what was, for a single morning, a thing to celebrate, was now a terror. I could tell she wasn't eating enough, but I also didn't care. I just wanted to hug her and never ever ever let go. The single thing I truly remember is her eyes. Empty, listless, she barely had the energy to hug me. She bit my shoulder, hard enough to draw a little blood. What she really wanted was to be fed by her mother or husband, but she couldn't stop herself from trying to get it even knowing that I couldn't give her what she needed. She cried and sobbed through her nose, even as she refused to let go with either her teeth or her arms. And then she fell asleep. It's that moment, that moment when my older sister, the one who who should have been supporting me, telling me what was just around the corner in my life, needed me, needed me, the little one, to tell the humans around us, what she needed, what my unborn niece needed.

And so when I walked over to her social worker, I didn't just put my sister down and walk away. I put her down, covered her ears, and then... feeling my throat rumble in determination and fear I forced my mouth open and meowed.

She wasn't the one who had decided we needed to be taken away. That was a position of authority, and hence paper work and age. No she was the poor college graduate, younger then I am as I write this. About my unkle's age actually. Not trained in biology, or intercultural work, or anything that could have prepared her for getting angrily meowed at by an 9 year old alien.

But for me, for us, meowing like that is reserved for telling an adult that someone (usually yourself) is injured or very sick. Its not cute, its closer to a scream of fear, but we don't have your Western Civilization with its cities of millions, and dangerous animals contained to zoos. My great grandmother was eaten by a Verduna, called by some of your people the Wolf-hipo, or greater horse-lion. We don't scream when we are scared. We meow.

And then once I had probably firmly placed myself in the category of very strange kid who should not be trusted with _anything_, I slowly, loudly,  trying to make it not a request, or a observation, but a command, said, "She needs cow-heart, butter, and penis."

Chapter 11: My Job.

That moment, staring my sister's case worker in the eye, putting my self, my family, at risk of angering the demigods that had ruined by life, because my sister needed me. That is when I started my job, the job I still have. To advocate for my people. Its not my sister that needs saving now. (She's incredibly happy and 'successful' by my reckoning. Very happy with Uncle, who she now refers to as her "ex husband" and is legally her wife. She is being a great parent to he 4 children they had together, and the 5 more children she fathered on Uncle's younger sisters, and is well on her way to founding her own clan like mother always wanted.)

But that doesn't mean I can stop. As modern medicine and pre-modern breeding patters collide, more and more catgirls leave our home timeline to try and find food and shelter in the Pan-Sentient-Community, (all too often just humans putting a "All species welcome sticker" on the door of a cafeteria that doesn't serve any non-vegan dairy.), and so new 'problems', new misunderstandings, new completely avoidable tragedies are happening every day.

Of course back then, back at the moment, would I have made the same choice if I knew just how hard it would be? Yes, actually, I think so. Maybe its vanity on my part but I do think I would have choose to give up being exactly the woman my mother expected of me, to face the challenges I do every day, to be, by my grandmother's standards a failure: I have only a single granddaughter and will likely never have more. I have had years where nothing I did seemed to make anything better, and it felt like the whole world hated me for daring to care about people, but.. I don't for a moment regret the choices I made, starting at that moment. I don't regret doing the right thing. If there's one thing I want you to take from this book, its not "Catgirls life cycles are fundamentally different then primate sapients", its not even "Maybe in a situation where you have no real idea what is going on, you shouldn't default to the most extrema action possible", it's "Its okay to do the right thing, even when it hurts".

The only one of the three things I demanded that my sister get that is actually _needed_ for our pregnancies is the butter. If you don't give us some proof that there is someone who our babies can nurse, then we _will_ miscarry. Its just not worth the risk. Butter is basically a way to trick our bodies into thinking there's more milk available then they are eating,, a super stimulus if you will, unaged cheese, or just a glass of whole milk every day will also work. As for the cow-heart, that's just that we A) always need more meat then you do, and B) think its the best "cut" of beef.

Despite what some of our healers will say, we don't _need_ to drink semen, and despite what others of our healers will say, no, we can't get any significant amount of nutrition though our vaginas. But when your unborn baby is trying to drain your life force through its belly button, or after throwing up when just smelling your mother's tea or roofing tar from three blocks away, is enough to make you vomit so much you feel like you might have given birth though your nose, being held by your husband is.. is very nice. (Mostly though we will just get constipaited and sleepy and kind of ... dumb.) but like racoons or bears, we crave varity and a wide range of food.
