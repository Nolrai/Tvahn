# Proto–South American Catgirl (PSA)

## Core Language Specification (“Conlang Bible”)

---

## 0. Status and Scope

* **PSA is a reconstructed proto-language**, comparable to PIE:

  * predates writing
  * reconstructed from daughter languages
  * reconstruction targets the language **immediately prior to a major expansion**
* Earlier speech stages existed but are **not fully reconstructible** and are treated as *pre-PSA*.
* PSA already contains **internal layering, erosion, and reanalysis** at the moment of reconstruction.

---

## 1. Fundamental Design Principle (Highest Priority)

### **Priority 1: Deixis / relevance / flow of attention**

PSA is not time-first, agent-first, or argument-first.

> **Everything is organized around whether something is within the current attentional field, and how attention flows through the event.**

This priority outranks:

* tense
* aspect
* mood
* grammatical relations
* noun class
* argument structure

If deixis is wrong, the clause is pragmatically invalid even if formally grammatical.

---

## 2. Noun Class System (Process Ontology)

### 2.1 Core Classes (Proto Stage)

PSA has **three primary noun classes**, encoding **process state**, not gender:

| Class         | Core meaning                                           |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Bearing**   | latent, growing, origin-state, protected               |
| **Traveling** | circulating, extracted, mobile, in-process             |
| **Nursing**   | completed, transformed, stabilized, dangerous / sacral |

These apply to:

* people
* animals
* food
* places
* tools
* abstractions

### 2.2 Marking

* **Traveling** is **unmarked** in PSA.
* **Bearing** marked by article **te**.
* **Nursing** marked by article **so**.
* Articles are:

  * free or clitic in PSA
  * later fuse as prefixes/suffixes in daughters

### 2.3 Diachronic Secondary Classes

Derived via morphology + noun class:

* diminutive + bearing → tools, children, inanimates
* plural + traveling → swarms, weather, rivers, forces
* augmentative + nursing → sacred/dangerous beings, mountains, old trees
* augmentative + bearing → land, origins, “the people”

Not all daughter languages retain these.

---

## 3. Deixis and Individuation (*si*)

### 3.1 Nature of *si*

* *si* is **not a definite article**
* It means:

  > **“this instance that is currently relevant / individuated”**
* Functions:

  * individuation of mass nouns
  * topical anchoring
  * override of default flow assumptions

Examples:

* *kita* → fish as commodity / circulation
* *si kita* → this piece/batch of fish
* *zo si kita* → this specific completed/dangerous batch

### 3.2 Pronouns

* Pronouns are **definite by default**
* Stacked forms (*desi, zosi*) behave as **gendered demonstratives**
* Pronoun systems later erode heavily.

---

## 4. Clause-Level Deixis (True Core System)

### 4.1 Historical Origin

* Descends from an **abandoned possessive / relational clitic**
* Originally meant:

  > “within my sphere / attached to me / under my responsibility”
* Possession collapsed as a semantic category.
* Morphology survived and generalized into **attention / relevance marking**.

---

### 4.2 Where Deixis Lives

Deixis is encoded in **three places**:

1. **Clause role marker** (mandatory)
2. **Verb fusion / light verbs**
3. **Relational adpositions**

NP-level *si* is only a residual shard of this system.

---

## 5. Adpositions as Deictic Paradigms

### 5.1 General System

* All pre/postpositions have **2–4 surface forms**
* These forms map onto **five abstract deictic levels**:

1. Very near speaker (strongly speaker-anchored)
2. Near addressee
3. Shared / discourse-near (“us”)
4. Default distance
5. Further than normal / backgrounded

Not all adpositions lexicalize all five.

---

### 5.2 Position Relative to Verb

* **Pre-verbal adpositions** = postpositions (sources, preconditions)
* **Post-verbal adpositions** = prepositions (goals, results)

---

### 5.3 Benefactive Example

Benefactive (“for / to”) paradigm:

* Speaker-proximal form:

  * means *for me*
  * does **not** take a noun
* Addressee-proximal form:

  * can mean *for you* alone
* Shared / neutral form:

  * default “for”
* Distal form:

  * institutions, future recipients, abstractions

Benefactive naturally drifts into **telic meaning**.

---

### 5.4 Instrumental Example

Instrumental has **two forms**:

* **“this” instrumental**

  * only if instrument is more bound to speaker than addressee/topic/context
* **“that” instrumental**

  * otherwise

Not distance-based; **sphere-of-control-based**.

---

## 6. Verb System Overview

### 6.1 No Agreement

* Verbs **never agree** with:

  * noun class
  * person
  * gender

* All relational meaning is elsewhere.

---

### 6.2 Dual TAM Systems (Both Real)

PSA has **two coexisting TAM systems**.

#### A. Older Morphological TAM (Still Productive)

* tense
* aspect
* mood
* conjunctive / dependency marking
* evidential overlays

Irregular, prestige-coded, never fully dies.

---

#### B. Newer TAM via Verb + Adposition + Deixis

* event-type verbs (“occur”, “move”, “exhaust”, etc.)
* relational adpositions
* deictic paradigms
* attentional closure vs openness

This system also encodes TAM — **independently**.

---

### 6.3 Result: Combinatorial Explosion

Because the two systems are:

* orthogonal
* overlapping
* historically accreted

They multiply into **~200 socially recognized clause types**, often called “tenses” by speakers.

Speakers:

* agree on distinctions
* rank them pragmatically
* cannot paraphrase them cleanly

---

## 7. Core Syntax: Event-Subject Construction

### 7.1 Structure

* **Verbal noun occupies subject position**
* No argument promotion occurs
* Agent, patient, instrument are obliques

This is **not a passive**.

---

### 7.2 Correct Classification

> **Event-subject / event-headed construction with agent demotion**

No object → subject promotion.

---

### 7.3 Example (Canonical)

> **painting sbj-that burnt-up by-you with-that brush for-me**
> “You used your brush to completely finish painting that for me.”

* Completion comes from **burn-up**
* Not from the construction itself
* Swap verb+adposition → different TAM

---

## 8. Subjecthood

* PSA lacks a stable grammatical subject
* In newer system:

  * events outrank agents
* Agents may be:

  * omitted
  * backgrounded
  * highlighted
* Responsibility ≠ syntactic prominence

---

## 9. Lexical Instability

* Certain semantic domains resist stable roots
* “Child” is unreconstructible:

  * replaced by metaphors (sling, burden, jewelry)
* Driven by:

  * noun class semantics
  * derivational competition
  * attentional framing

---

## 10. Phonology

### 10.1 Vowels

**Targets (monophthongs):**

* /a i u ɪ ə/

**Nasal (phonemic, restricted):**

* /ã ɪ̃ ũ/

**Length (unstable, stressed only):**

* /aː uː/

Most stressed syllables are **vowel trajectories** (VV, Vj, Vw, Vr).

---

### 10.2 Consonants (Underlying ~14)

* /p t k ts s h m n l r j w ʔ/
* No phonemic voicing
* Heavy allophony

---

### 10.3 Allophony

* Palatalization before /i ɪ j/
* Intervocalic weakening (taps, glottals)
* **Coda /N/** = nasal archiphoneme (Japanese-like)
* No geminates

---

### 10.4 Glottal Stop

* /ʔ/ is **not a coda**
* Marks **prosodic word boundaries**
* Appears word-initially before:

  * vowels
  * liquids
  * sibilants
* Dropped in compounds / lexicalized phrases

---

### 10.5 Phonotactics

* Preferred syllables: (ʔ)CV, (ʔ)CVC
* No clusters
* Complexity in nuclei, not edges

**Register effects:**

* Formal: lexeme-faithful endings
* Casual:

  * nouns open (CV)
  * verbs closed (CVC with /N/)

---

## 11. Diachronic Tendencies

* Articles fuse
* Pronouns erode
* Length → diphthongs/liquids
* New TAM expands
* Old TAM fossilizes but survives
* Daughter languages diverge sharply

---

## 12. One-Sentence Core Definition

> **Proto–South American Catgirl is a relevance-first, event-headed language with a process-based noun class system, dual overlapping TAM architectures, and a deictic attentional grammar that replaces agreement and dominates clause structure.**
