## Linguistic Periodization

* **PPSA / Orinoco Horizon (~4000-3000 BID):**
  Earlier related speech varieties before full homogenization. Important for design reconstruction, but not recoverable in-universe as a single stable language.

* **PSA Proper (~2300-1800? BID):**
  The reconstructible common ancestor. This corresponds to broad regional linguistic and material-cultural commonality after the emergence of cities, before major daughter-language divergence.

* **Post-PSA / Early Daughter Continuum (~1900-400 BID):**
  PSA-descended varieties spread and differentiate, including the ancestors of Sahumãk, A recognisable group called the Golden family decends from speakers living in OTL Costa Rica and Panama.

* **Tar-Bird Horizon / Pre-Classical Sahumank (~400 BID-50 AID):**
  A archelogicaly distinct 'horizon' starts around OTL San Juan river and OTL lake Nicuagua, showing farming of animal feed, and aquaculture, as well as the euponimous "Tar-Birds", bird shaped impresions, carvings, and mumifications covered or composed of asphalt like tar, or black water proof shelacs. They are clearly simular to the earlier and contemporus material culture in OTL Costa Rica but are also very distinct.
  Pre-Classical Sahumank exists an speech comunity mostly isolated from other PSA descended languages. Texile writing system undergoes heavy simplification and phonolozation eventually becoming a hemi-sylabatry, these are depicted in durable media, but the writing form does not transfer well, being effectively a form of psudo-graphia just depicting the form of the weaving, rather then being readable. There does seem to have been somewhat extensive experimentation with trying to make readible depictions of texile writing, but the attemts to mirror texture or three d shape don't work well.

* **Classical Sahumãk (~0-370 AID):**
  Attested written daughter language. Sudden durable inscriptions likely reflect transfer from older textile literacy, not invention of writing. The major inovation is the creation of straight line glyphs replacing patterns of which thread is woven above the 'weft', and how the weft is locked to the next row. Notes on both systems are in the Sahumank folder.

## Misc. Notes for reorganization later.

  PSA is an externally a priori language: it is not descended from or modeled on any real-world language family. Internally, however, it is treated as a reconstructed proto-language, comparable in cultural importance to Proto-Indo-European before the Age of Sail. It is the reconstructed ancestor of several languages spoken by the Catgirls of southern Middle America and northern South America. One known daughter language, Sahumank, will serve as an internal reference point for future reconstruction and family design.

  Tvahn (an modern era name for the world) is wetter and cooler than Earth, with smaller deserts and larger forests. Tvahn is technically an alternate history, but more accurately alternate natural history. The main divergence occurs in the Mesozoic, after monotremes and therian mammals split. The dominant later mammalian branch evolved a staged reproductive system tying nursing, fertility, and male gamete production together, meaning reproduction cannot occur through a lone mother. This biology strongly shapes PSA’s grammar, especially its process-based noun classes: Bearing, Traveling, and Nursing.
  PSA speakers live in a riverine, forested region (The Orinoco vally at first then expanding west and north) where fishing, wetland animals, early pig-analog management, and household obligation are central. The language is reconstructed for the period just before a major expansion, likely connected to improved fish and pig farming, river travel, and growing inter-household ritual networks.

  Two closely replated sapient species share this world. Their relationship resembles a caste-like social arrangement, though it is less directly exploitative than comparable human systems because their evolved social rewards encourage interspecies cooperation more than direct competition. The smaller A. minorus often appears uncomfortably subservient to American human observers, and this relationship may influence PSA’s deixis, address systems, obligation vocabulary, and social registers. Comunities have been mixed since predating A. minor's develoupment of speach, so usually any variation between speices is closer to class based "dialects" or "private languages" among an extended family then true seperate languages, and even that level of difference is almost always a skew in 'register' use rather than a difference in capabilities. That is in a given region the linguistic varriation is larly between locations and households, not split into A. minor and M. major comunities.
  'Housholds' are large by human standards, 5 is the minimum realistic size, and >10 is more normal. They don't really pair bond the way we do, instead a given group is generaly fairly promiscous with sex being more about social status and comfort then either an pair bond or herem like system. This varies less then it does in humans, but this should still be considered just the standard base line, the way small extended families is the human default.

  PSA speakers are short-muzzled mammals with carnivore-descended dentition, but their lips, tongue, and jaw control support broadly humanlike articulation. Their front teeth and lips permit bilabials and rounded vowels, while their non-primate tooth pattern makes labiodentals and dental fricatives rare or absent. PSA therefore favors bilabials, alveolar/postalveolar stops and affricates, velars, nasals, liquids, glides, /s h/, and glottal contrasts rather than fine dental or labiodental contrasts.

  ## phonotactic notes from examining the PPSA_to_PSA.lsc file

  PSA strongly prefers open or simple closed syllables, with no ordinary consonant clusters. The normal surface syllable shapes are V, CV, VC, and CVC, though V and VC mostly occur word-initially or through erosion. The affricate /ts/ behaves as a single consonant, not a cluster.

  Complexity is concentrated in the nucleus rather than the margins. High vowels become glides beside other vowels, producing surface /j w/ and vowel-glide sequences. Older long vowels and vowel sequences often become diphthongs or glide-vowel transitions.

  Final nasals are unstable. Earlier /n/ nasalizes the preceding vowel and is then lost word-finally or before consonants, producing nasal vowels /ã ĩ ũ/. Coda consonants survive best as oral stops, /s/, /m/, /k/, /t/, and fossilized remnants of older nasal clusters.

  The glottal stop /ʔ/ marks prosodic word boundaries and vowel-initial words. It is not a normal coda and is deleted or suppressed near consonants.
