```text
1   lip
2   makrul
3   hu
4   sat
5   salp
6   sakrul
7   satxu
8   makrat
9   makralp
10  makramak
11  makru
12  hut / rolip hut

13  hut lip
etc.

24  romak hut
25  romak hut lip
etc.

36  rou hut
37  rou hut lip
etc.

48  rot hut
49  rot hut lip
etc.

60  rolp hut
61  rolp hut lip
etc.

72  sakrul-rol hut
73  sakrul-rol hut lip
etc.

96  makrat-rol hut
97  makrat-rol hut lip
etc.

132 makru-rol hut
133 makru-rol hut lip
etc.

144 tþuwx
145 tþuwx lip
etc.
```

Sets of twelve use the **LONG** class counter. This is why both **rolip hut** “one dozen” and **makrat-rol hut** “eight dozen” use LONG-class forms.

Conventionally, the collective form is used for dozen-counting, even though many other LONG-class objects would use the paucal for groups up to eleven.

Bare numerals are rare. They are mostly used for emphasis, or when talking about numerals themselves. In ordinary counting, or when talking about sets of objects, PWT uses counters. These counters are also used pronominally.

Technically, what I gloss as **COLL** is closer to a plural. However, since this behaves differently from plural marking in languages like English, I use **COLL** for “collective” instead.

---

## LONG-class counters

```text
1   rolip
2   romak
3   rou
4   rot
5   rolp

Paucal: rouku
COLL:   rol
```

For numbers above five, the bare numeral modifies either the paucal or collective counter.
