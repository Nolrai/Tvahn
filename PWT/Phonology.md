# Proto-Wind-Tribe (PWT) Phonology and Phonotactics Summary

## General Character

PWT is intended to sound:

* Moderately large in consonant inventory.
* Rich in codas and short roots.
* Fairly rugged, with several unusual dorsal and dental sounds.
* Strongly head-final and isolating, but with enough phonological texture to support later daughter-language divergence.

---

# Phonemic Inventory

## Vowels

PWT has a simple five-vowel system:

| Front | Central | Back |
| ----- | ------- | ---- |
| i     | a       | u    |
| e     |         | o    |

No phonemic vowel length, tone, or phonation distinctions have yet been established.

## Consonants

### Stops

| Bilabial | Alveolar | Palato-postalveolar | Velar |
| -------- | -------- | ------------------- | ----- |
| p        | t        | c                   | k     |
| b        | d        | j                   | g     |

### Affricates

| Alveolar | Dental | Velar |
| -------- | ------ | ----- |
| ts       | tþ     | kx    |

* **tþ** is a dental affricate, approximately [t̪θ].
* **kx** is a dorsal affricate or strongly fricated velar release, approximately [k͡x].

# The Palato-Postalveolar Column

PWT treats one articulatory region as a single phonological category whose realization depends on manner of articulation.

| Manner    | Realization |
| --------- | ----------- |
| Stop      | c, j        |
| Fricative | ʃ           |
| Nasal     | ñ           |
| Glide     | y           |

This is a single place feature in the language's organization rather than a set of unrelated consonants.

This column is expected to evolve differently in daughter languages.

# Phonotactics

## Preferred Root Shapes

Common root patterns:

* CV
* CVC
* CVCV
* CCVC
* CVCVC
* CVCCV

The language favors relatively short roots.

---

## Syllable Structure

General template:

```text
(C)(C)V(C)(C)
```

Not every cluster is permitted, and heavy syllables are less common than light ones.

# Distributional Tendencies

* CVC roots are extremely common.
* Longer clusters are comparatively rare and therefore perceptually salient.
* Very short roots (CV and some CVC roots) are likely to become:

  * pronouns,
  * particles,
  * postpositions,
  * grammatical markers.

---

# Phonological Character

PWT should sound:

* compact,
* consonant-rich,
* somewhat rough and earthy,
* suited to riverbanks, storms, reefs, and trade ports,
* not especially "windy" in its proto stage.

The later association of Wind Tribe cultures with mountains, highlands, and wind symbolism is a historical development rather than an original feature of PWT.
