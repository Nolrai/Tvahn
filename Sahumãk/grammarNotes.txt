


Each of these takes one of the discourse states:

Atemporal - Is used for gnomic sentences, habituals, infitives, and gerunds, when used for physically distant things it implies shared knowledge. These clauses are invisible for the purposes of how the other three "tenses" interact in a conversation/narative.

Continual - Used to continue a narative, or start a section describing the here and now.

Remote - Used to start a section that takes place in the past, or to report on distant or suprising events.

departing - Used to end a section. If the next clause is Continual then that means the narative focus is back in the present, if the next clause is Remote, then it is moving to different remote location, or past event.

The 4 forms are listed in order: atemporal (atem), continual (cont), remote, departing (depart).
