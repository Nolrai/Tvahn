# Catgirls Timeline — Working Checkpoints

## c. 15,000 BDI — Post-Climate Stabilization Ecology

### Purpose
Establish the stabilized late-glacial/early-interglacial ecological baseline before dense settlement, proto-agriculture, or major built environments.

### Global Ecology
Strong, stable Pacific and Atlantic ocean currents carry more heat toward the poles. This produces a warmer global climate overall, especially at high latitudes, while moderating tropical heat.

Sea levels are higher than during the ATL glacial period. Forests expand, deserts contract, and many temperate/coastal environments become more productive than their OTL equivalents.

Antarctica is much warmer than OTL, but remains too cold for plants. Ice still dominates the continent, though ocean-reaching ice sheets are reduced.

### Catgirl Distribution
Catgirls live on every continent except Oceania and Antarctica.

Major-to-minor ratios vary regionally, roughly from 1:3 to 1:7.

### Historical Significance
This is the ecological starting point for later divergence. Higher forest cover, richer coasts, and reduced deserts create broader zones of stable high-density foraging than in OTL.

## c. 7,000 BDI — Before First Proto-Cities Fully Crystallize

### Purpose
Show the world before true agricultural urbanism, when population density is rising and some regions are approaching permanent settlement.

### Population and Settlement
Population densities are increasing in several highly productive regions, especially:

- South China
- The Nile basin
- South American tropical river systems
- Southern Japan
- The American Great Plains

Permanent or semi-permanent settlements exist in stable high-density biomes, especially where reliable aquatic resources support predictable food supply. Important examples include salmon runs, eel systems, clam reefs, estuaries, and rich riverine fisheries.

### Subsistence
Intentional environmental management is widespread, but most regions have not yet crossed into full agriculture.

Catgirls are reshaping landscapes through burning, selective planting, animal management, fish traps, wetland modification, and other practices, but these systems are still mixed subsistence ecologies rather than true farming economies.

### Migration
Catgirls have reached Australia by this point: Australian inhabitation is now becoming established.

### Historical Significance
This is the pre-urban threshold. Some societies are becoming sedentary or near-sedentary without yet depending on agriculture. The key pressure is not “farming begins,” but “food systems become predictable enough that settlement can become permanent.”

## c. 5,000 BDI — Orinoco First-City World

### Purpose
Mark the emergence of the first true city-like settlements in the Orinoco system, driven by integrated aquaculture and managed animal production.

### Orinoco System
Aquaculture and river-pig pens are built into shared multi-use riverbank structures. These systems require and enable permanent settlement along parts of the Orinoco river system.

Fish, wetland foods, managed river pigs, and constructed river-edge infrastructure form a dense subsistence base. This creates one of the first regions where settlement, food production, ritual space, and labor coordination begin to fuse into proto-urban life.

### Wider Tropical River Systems
Aquaculture is developing in many tropical river systems, but in most places it has not yet become the staple basis of diet.

The Orinoco is ahead because animal pens, fish management, and built river-edge infrastructure reinforce each other.

### Eurasia
Megalithic structures begin appearing in parts of Eurasia. These do not necessarily imply cities or agriculture. They show rising ritual coordination, territorial memory, and labor organization in some high-density or seasonally aggregated societies.

### Historical Significance
This checkpoint establishes that the first city-world is not grain-based. It emerges from river engineering, aquaculture, and managed animal calories rather than cereal agriculture.

# c. 3,500 BDI

## Orinoco River System

Aquaculute and animals fed by intentionally grown crops has replaced the earlier farrow/gathered/release and recapture systems in densly ocupied areas, and wild populations of river-pig have been pushed into marginal habitats, or hunted out.

By this point, the Orinoco system has long been occupied by many proto-cities. Small coalitions have occasionally managed to rule over two or three cities at a time, but no polity has successfully unified the wider river system.

Instead, the region is divided into a patchwork of allied families, rival houses, shrine obligations, marriage-like foster ties, trade partnerships, and old ritual claims. Many important families have branches in multiple cities, while each city contains several significant families with overlapping external loyalties.

This is close to the default multi-settlement civilizational structure for catgirls. Political order does not naturally condense into a single king, palace, capital, or patrilineal dynasty. It more often forms as a braided network of creches, senior families, oath-lines, shrine offices, and city councils.

The word glossed in English as “king” often really means something closer to “ruling family,” “first house,” or “senior creche.” For example, in Sahumank and the Golden Empire, the relevant title literally means “first family of the five-family house.” The important political unit is the creche, not the patriline.

The term is still glossed as “king” because it fills a similar narrative role: outsiders speak of “the king,” chronicles name “the king,” and political stories use the office as a symbol of rule. But the underlying social dynamics are fundamentally different. A catgirl “king” is not usually a singular hereditary patriarch. It is more often a ruling household, an office-bearing creche, or the public face of a senior family coalition.

## Other South (and middle) America

The pacific high-lands are slowly shifting towards pastorialism, and small intensive aquaculture comunities exist where there can be built onto rivers, but are still a small part of of the overall economy, and serve more as suplimental food sources to an outpost or wintering station.

The river-pig-pen/aquaculture package has spread to the northern parts of the amazon basin and up the alantic middle american coast, but settlements are still very small compared to the city and village network in Orinco and often mostly the less land efficient but lower labor herb gardens + and semi-wild pastures left follow, not planted as the feed stock for the pigs and fishes.

# Eurasian Wetland and Riverine Emergence

By this point, several Eurasian and North African river systems are developing dense settlement traditions, but not along the same hierarchy as OTL.

The strongest early candidates are wet, fish-rich, fodder-rich lowlands: the Pearl and lower Yangzi systems, the Ganges-Brahmaputra basin, the Nile floodplain and delta, the Mekong and Red River systems, and the Mesopotamian marsh-river complex.

These regions support dense catgirl populations through aquaculture, penned animals, wetland hunting, bird management, fish traps, shellfish, reeds, fodder plants, coppiced woodland, and managed forest-edge ecologies.

The Nile and Southwest Asia remain important, but they are not uniquely privileged as the first centers of civilization. Their later importance comes partly from administrative geography, dryland pastoralism, trade corridors, and eventually metallurgy.

## Bronze Is Not Equally Revolutionary Everywhere

Bronze does not have the same historical force in every region.

In the Golden/Mesoamerican world, bronze enters an already literate, urban, shrine-centered, state-forming civilization. Its main effects are on prestige goods, ritual sound objects, elite tools, tribute, craft specialization, and long-distance exchange. It matters, but it does not create cities, writing, states, or imperial ideology.

In Southwest Asia and parts of Eurasia, bronze is more disruptive. There, it combines with pastoral mobility, trade corridors, weapon production, woodworking, transport, and competitive state formation. Bronze becomes part of a broader military-logistical transformation.

So “Bronze Age” should not be a global stage. It is a regional technological pressure whose importance depends on what social systems already exist when it arrives.

| Region                                  | Bronze impact                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Mesoamerica / Golden world              | Important prestige, craft, tribute, ritual, and specialist-tool technology; not civilizationally foundational                                                      |
| Andes / western South America analogues | Potentially more structurally important if metalworking ties into highland-lowland exchange, state storage, and elite display                                      |
| Southwest Asia / Eurasian corridors     | Much more disruptive because bronze interacts with pastoral mobility, long-distance trade, weapons, plow/woodworking systems, and competitive inter-polity warfare |

## Oceanic Separation at Present Day

The present day, c. 2800 ADI, is still far before routine trans-Atlantic navigation.

Although the Americas contain very old literate civilizations and the first continental-scale empires are beginning to form, the Atlantic remains a true civilizational barrier. Eurasia and the Americas are not yet part of a single world-system.

In technological analogy, the present day is closer to an Iron Age / early imperial-pressure world than to an Age of Discovery world. Routine trans-Atlantic shipping is still roughly 1500 years in the future.

As a result, the setting’s present has continental empires, literate archives, iron weapons, long-distance overland and coastal trade, and major religious-political traditions — but not global oceanic empires, Atlantic colonization, or hemispheric exchange.
