Proto–South American Catgirl (PSA)

Core Language Specification (“Conlang Bible”)

0. Status and Scope

  • PSA is a reconstructed proto-language, comparable to PIE:

    • predates writing
    • reconstructed from daughter languages
    • reconstruction targets the language immediately prior to a major expansion

  • Earlier speech stages existed but are not fully reconstructible and are treated as pre-PSA.

  • PSA already contains internal layering, erosion, and reanalysis at the moment of reconstruction.

1. Fundamental Design Principle (Highest Priority)

Priority 1: Deixis / relevance / flow of attention

PSA is not time-first, agent-first, or argument-first.

Everything is organized around whether something is within the current attentional field, and how attention flows through the event.

This priority outranks:

  • tense
  • aspect
  • mood
  • grammatical relations
  • noun class
  • argument structure

If deixis is wrong, the clause is pragmatically invalid even if formally grammatical.

2. Noun Class System (Process Ontology)

2.1 Core Classes (Proto Stage)

PSA has three primary noun classes, encoding process state, not gender:

Class Core meaning
Bearing latent, growing, origin-state, protected
Traveling circulating, extracted, mobile, in-process
Nursing completed, transformed, stabilized, dangerous / sacral

These apply to:

  • people
  • animals
  • food
  • places
  • tools
  • abstractions

2.2 Marking

  • Traveling is unmarked in PSA.

  • Bearing marked by article te.

  • Nursing marked by article so.

  • Articles are:

    • free or clitic in PSA
    • later fuse as prefixes/suffixes in daughters

2.3 Diachronic Secondary Classes

Derived via morphology + noun class:

  • diminutive + bearing → tools, children, inanimates
  • plural + traveling → swarms, weather, rivers, forces
  • augmentative + nursing → sacred/dangerous beings, mountains, old trees
  • augmentative + bearing → land, origins, “the people”

Not all daughter languages retain these.

3. Deixis and Individuation (si)

3.1 Nature of si

  • si is not a definite article

  • It means:

    “this instance that is currently relevant / individuated”

  • Functions:

    • individuation of mass nouns
    • topical anchoring
    • override of default flow assumptions

Examples:

  • kita → fish as commodity / circulation
  • si kita → this piece/batch of fish
  • zo si kita → this specific completed/dangerous batch

3.2 Pronouns

  • Pronouns are definite by default
  • Stacked forms (desi, zosi) behave as gendered demonstratives
  • Pronoun systems later erode heavily.

4. Clause-Level Deixis (True Core System)

4.1 Historical Origin

  • Descends from an abandoned possessive / relational clitic

  • Originally meant:

    “within my sphere / attached to me / under my responsibility”

  • Possession collapsed as a semantic category.

  • Morphology survived and generalized into attention / relevance marking.

4.2 Where Deixis Lives

Deixis is encoded in three places:

  1. Clause role marker (mandatory)
  2. Verb fusion / light verbs
  3. Relational adpositions

NP-level si is only a residual shard of this system.

5. Adpositions as Deictic Paradigms

5.1 General System

  • All pre/postpositions have 2–4 surface forms
  • These forms map onto five abstract deictic levels:
  1. Very near speaker (strongly speaker-anchored)
  2. Near addressee
  3. Shared / discourse-near (“us”)
  4. Default distance
  5. Further than normal / backgrounded

Not all adpositions lexicalize all five.

5.2 Position Relative to Verb

  • Pre-verbal adpositions = postpositions (sources, preconditions)
  • Post-verbal adpositions = prepositions (goals, results)

5.3 Benefactive Example

Benefactive (“for / to”) paradigm:

  • Speaker-proximal form:

    • means for me
    • does not take a noun

  • Addressee-proximal form:

    • can mean for you alone

  • Shared / neutral form:

    • default “for”

  • Distal form:

    • institutions, future recipients, abstractions

Benefactive naturally drifts into telic meaning.

5.4 Instrumental Example

Instrumental has two forms:

  • “this” instrumental

    • only if instrument is more bound to speaker than addressee/topic/context

  • “that” instrumental

    • otherwise

Not distance-based; sphere-of-control-based.

6. Verb System Overview

6.1 No Agreement

  • Verbs never agree with:

    • noun class
    • person
    • gender

  • All relational meaning is elsewhere.

6.2 Dual TAM Systems (Both Real)

PSA has two coexisting TAM systems.

A. Older Morphological TAM (Still Productive)

  • tense
  • aspect
  • mood
  • conjunctive / dependency marking
  • evidential overlays

Irregular, prestige-coded, never fully dies.

B. Newer TAM via Verb + Adposition + Deixis

  • event-type verbs (“occur”, “move”, “exhaust”, etc.)
  • relational adpositions
  • deictic paradigms
  • attentional closure vs openness

This system also encodes TAM — independently.

6.3 Result: Combinatorial Explosion

Because the two systems are:

  • orthogonal
  • overlapping
  • historically accreted

They multiply into ~200 socially recognized clause types, often called “tenses” by speakers.

Speakers:

  • agree on distinctions
  • rank them pragmatically
  • cannot paraphrase them cleanly

7. Core Syntax: Event-Subject Construction

7.1 Structure

  • Verbal noun occupies subject position
  • No argument promotion occurs
  • Agent, patient, instrument are obliques

This is not a passive.

7.2 Correct Classification

Event-subject / event-headed construction with agent demotion

No object → subject promotion.

7.3 Example (Canonical)

painting sbj-that burnt-up by-you with-that brush for-me “You used your brush to completely finish painting that for me.”

  • Completion comes from burn-up
  • Not from the construction itself
  • Swap verb+adposition → different TAM

8. Subjecthood

  • PSA lacks a stable grammatical subject

  • In newer system:

    • events outrank agents

  • Agents may be:

    • omitted
    • backgrounded
    • highlighted

  • Responsibility ≠ syntactic prominence

9. Lexical Instability

  • Certain semantic domains resist stable roots

  • “Child” is unreconstructible:

    • replaced by metaphors (sling, burden, jewelry)

  • Driven by:

    • noun class semantics
    • derivational competition
    • attentional framing

10. Phonology

10.1 Vowels

Targets (monophthongs):

  • /a i u ɪ ə/

Nasal (phonemic, restricted):

  • /ã ɪ̃ ũ/

Length (unstable, stressed only):

  • /aː uː/

Most stressed syllables are vowel trajectories (VV, Vj, Vw, Vr).

10.2 Consonants (Underlying ~14)

  • /p t k ts s h m n l r j w ʔ/
  • No phonemic voicing
  • Heavy allophony

10.3 Allophony

  • Palatalization before /i ɪ j/
  • Intervocalic weakening (taps, glottals)
  • Coda /N/ = nasal archiphoneme (Japanese-like)
  • No geminates

10.4 Glottal Stop

  • /ʔ/ is not a coda

  • Marks prosodic word boundaries

  • Appears word-initially before:

    • vowels
    • liquids
    • sibilants

  • Dropped in compounds / lexicalized phrases

10.5 Phonotactics

  • Preferred syllables: (ʔ)CV, (ʔ)CVC
  • No clusters
  • Complexity in nuclei, not edges

Register effects:

  • Formal: lexeme-faithful endings

  • Casual:

    • nouns open (CV)
    • verbs closed (CVC with /N/)

11. Diachronic Tendencies

  • Articles fuse
  • Pronouns erode
  • Length → diphthongs/liquids
  • New TAM expands
  • Old TAM fossilizes but survives
  • Daughter languages diverge sharply

12. One-Sentence Core Definition

Proto–South American Catgirl is a relevance-first, event-headed language with a process-based noun class system, dual overlapping TAM architectures, and a deictic attentional grammar that replaces agreement and dominates clause structure.

PPSA Root Lexicon (Working Canon)

This document collects the current canon roots and key derivational class markers defined for Proto-PSA (PPSA), plus selected high-priority derived compounds, kinship terms, and core relational constructions.

Noun-Class / Life-Stage Markers

  • te- = Bearing class (early / fertile / initiating / trickster / smallish)
  • Ø (unmarked) = Traveling class (moving / ambitious / active default)
  • so- = Nursing class (completed / containing / nurturing / respected / commanding)

Historical note: The prefix te- did not originally exist as an independent morpheme. It was later reanalyzed from the word tesint (see below). Only after this reanalysis did te- become a productive bearing-class marker.

Class marking is usually lexicalized, but in some kinship terms it may remain semantically productive (e.g., te-sikimani vs sikimani vs so-sikimani).

Core Roots (Canon)

Terrain & Materials

  • hu = water (default / flowing)
  • astro = earth / ground / floor / forest (general terrain)
  • iman = breast
  • tsan = textile / rope / woven material
  • pak = big-toe claw / kicking claw (historically also stake/post)
  • se = wood / branch (material head in compounds)

Animals & Body Substances

  • ram = domestic pig (pig-analog; aquatic eutransdontid)
  • ʔhas = fish (generic)
  • ʔrom = fat / grease
  • sorom = rancid / spoiled / gone bad (especially of food, grease, or preserved matter)
  • ʔank = bone
  • upon = (semi-)edible plant matter (leaf / tuber / nut / grain; not wood)
  • pum = hand claw (deep semantic category; distinct from pak ‘foot claw’)

Core Verbs / Light Verbs

  • mus = give
  • sik = take
  • kemi = use / employ
  • kemitsotan = speak / use breath / speak aloud
  • luma = general motion verb (go / come / carry / throw / enter / leave; meaning determined by relational marking)
  • lante = settle / lower oneself
  • ʔen = rise / stand
  • rokunk = hunt
  • utun = home (dwelling / place of return; distinct from built structure)
  • ronst = cook / apply fire (base for boil / roast / smoke / salt compounds)
  • u:kani = push against / clutch (later evolves into PSA event-frame verb miw wukani)

Social / Relational Roots

  • tesint = belly → later “person who was pregnant (with me).” This form is the historical source of the te- prefix. Only later was tesint reanalyzed folk-etymologically as te-sint, allowing te- to spread as a productive bearing marker. The reduced element sint is sometimes used poetically to mean “catgirl,” but never becomes the default term.

  • mank = obligation / debt (final nk realized [ŋk] → [ŋ]; emphatic reduplicated form mankank /maŋgaŋ/ bleached into basic lexical form)

Core Relational Markers (PPSA)

  • miw = upward / above / upright / superior orientation (structurally central adposition)
  • pai = interior / between / within relation
  • kun = toward / to
  • ʔrap = away from / from
  • kent = by holding / carried-by
  • kept = by propelling / thrown-by

(Note: In motion constructions, the carried/thrown entity receives topic marking.)

Selected Derived Lexemes (PPSA Stage)

Water System

  • river = hu-hu → huhu
  • pond / containing water = so-hu → sohu

Boundaries / Enclosures

  • boundary / bank = tsan-pak → tsanpak
  • fence / enclosure (wood as head; containing boundary) = se-so-tsanpak → sesotsanpak

Landforms

  • hill = iman-astro → imanastro (“earth-breast,” breast as head)
  • open ground / meadow = astro-so-hu → astrosohu (earth-pond logic)
  • mud = so-hu-ram → sohuram (pond-pig logic)
  • stone = astro-astro → astrostro

Dwelling & Fire

  • house (built structure) = so-se-so-se → sosesose (archetypal “finished wood,” reduced form)
  • hearth = so-se-ronst → soseronst (finished wood-fire place)
  • mayor / village head / lord = so-se-so-se-tsene → sosesosetsene (“house-name,” the house by which the settlement is known; later broadens toward “ruler”)

Fishing & Hunting Toolkit

  • hook for fish = pum-ʔhas → pumahas
  • trap / snare = tsan-(u)-rokunk → tsanurokunk
  • butcher / cut meat = rokunk-utun → rokunkutun

Feeding & Exchange

  • feed (animals) = mus-upon → musupon
  • trade = mus-sik → musisik (give-take)
  • crush / grind = astro-stro-kimi → astrostrokimi (“stone-use,” i.e. reduce by working against stone)

Kinship Terms

  • woman who bore me = tesint (historically unanalyzable at earliest stage; only later reinterpreted as te-sint)

  • woman who nursed me = so-kemi-iman → sokemiman (nursing breast-user)

  • child (actual child stage) = te-kemi-iman-i → tekemimani (bearing breast-user-DIM)

  • younger / same-age sibling (traveling stage default) = sik-iman-i → sikimani (breast-taker-DIM)

    • may appear as te-sikimani (young child stage)
    • may appear as so-sikimani (elder reference)

  • older sibling = mus-iman → musiman (breast-giver)

  • guest = kemi-huhu → kemihuhu (river-user)

Motion & Posture Constructions

  • go / come toward = luma kun
  • go / leave from = luma ʔrap
  • carry (by holding) = luma kent
  • throw (by propelling) = luma kept
  • settle / sit / calm = lante pai
  • sit upright = lante miw
  • stand up = ʔen miw

Cooking

  • boil = ronst-hu → ronstuhu
  • roast / bake (dry cook; fire-fire) = ronst-ronst → ronstonst
  • salt = ronst-astro-so-huhu → ronstastrosohuhu
  • smoke = ronst-astro-ronst → ronstastroronst

Body & Vitality

  • blood = ro
  • breath = tsotan
  • hand = nen
  • foot = amant
  • hunger = nepant
  • sleep = sun

Weather & Time

  • rain = miw-hu → miwhu (water from above)
  • dry / drought = hanst
  • wind = etna
  • cold (noun) = etna-hu → etnahu (wet-wind)
  • cold (adjectival construction) = [noun] etnahu kent iru (cold-carrying; with free relativizer iru)
  • dawn = atup
  • night = hetnos

Speech, Law & the Unseen

  • name / word = tsene
  • oath = tsene-mank → tsenemank (name of debt)
  • taboo / prohibition / mistake = tsan-ro → tsanro (blood-rope)
  • forbidden = pai tsanro
  • ancestor = lexicalized compound from “nursing breast-user (burned) within” → provisional form sokemimanpai (historically cremation-based; exact reduction to be determined)
  • spirit = uuki miw iru (they who lurk above; iru present in PPSA as free relativizing element, not yet fused)

  • Nicaragua = sohuhumanki (“the promised river”; lexicalized place-name)

Structural Notes

  • iru exists in PPSA as an independent relativizing / clause-linking element; fusion and suffixal behavior develop later in PSA.
  • iiru exists in PPSA is just iru with emphasis but early in the transition to PSA it becomes lexicalized as a interogative pronoun, before fusing onto the preposition along with the other “persons/distances”.
  • Compounding rule: head-first, with a strong tendency for material heads (e.g., se-…).
  • Life-stage markers (te- / Ø / so-) are derivational noun-class prefixes tied to cultural life stages.
  • In some kinship terms, class marking may remain semantically productive rather than strictly lexicalized.
  • Relational meaning is largely carried by adpositions rather than verb roots.
  • PPSA compounds are morphologically transparent; opacity emerges later in PSA through sound change.

Linguistic Periodization

  • PPSA / Orinoco Horizon (~4000-3000 BID): Earlier related speech varieties before full homogenization. Important for design reconstruction, but not recoverable in-universe as a single stable language.

  • PSA Proper (~2300-1800? BID): The reconstructible common ancestor. This corresponds to broad regional linguistic and material-cultural commonality after the emergence of cities, before major daughter-language divergence.

  • Post-PSA / Early Daughter Continuum (~1900-400 BID): PSA-descended varieties spread and differentiate, including the ancestors of Sahumãk, A recognisable group called the Golden family decends from speakers living in OTL Costa Rica and Panama.

  • Tar-Bird Horizon / Pre-Classical Sahumank (~400 BID-50 AID): A archelogicaly distinct ‘horizon’ starts around OTL San Juan river and OTL lake Nicuagua, showing farming of animal feed, and aquaculture, as well as the euponimous “Tar-Birds”, bird shaped impresions, carvings, and mumifications covered or composed of asphalt like tar, or black water proof shelacs. They are clearly simular to the earlier and contemporus material culture in OTL Costa Rica but are also very distinct. Pre-Classical Sahumank exists an speech comunity mostly isolated from other PSA descended languages. Texile writing system undergoes heavy simplification and phonolozation eventually becoming a hemi-sylabatry, these are depicted in durable media, but the writing form does not transfer well, being effectively a form of psudo-graphia just depicting the form of the weaving, rather then being readable. There does seem to have been somewhat extensive experimentation with trying to make readible depictions of texile writing, but the attemts to mirror texture or three d shape don’t work well.

  • Classical Sahumãk (~0-370 AID): Attested written daughter language. Sudden durable inscriptions likely reflect transfer from older textile literacy, not invention of writing. The major inovation is the creation of straight line glyphs replacing patterns of which thread is woven above the ‘weft’, and how the weft is locked to the next row. Notes on both systems are in the Sahumank folder.

Misc. Notes for reorganization later.

PSA is an externally a priori language: it is not descended from or modeled on any real-world language family. Internally, however, it is treated as a reconstructed proto-language, comparable in cultural importance to Proto-Indo-European before the Age of Sail. It is the reconstructed ancestor of several languages spoken by the Catgirls of southern Middle America and northern South America. One known daughter language, Sahumank, will serve as an internal reference point for future reconstruction and family design.

Tvahn (an modern era name for the world) is wetter and cooler than Earth, with smaller deserts and larger forests. Tvahn is technically an alternate history, but more accurately alternate natural history. The main divergence occurs in the Mesozoic, after monotremes and therian mammals split. The dominant later mammalian branch evolved a staged reproductive system tying nursing, fertility, and male gamete production together, meaning reproduction cannot occur through a lone mother. This biology strongly shapes PSA’s grammar, especially its process-based noun classes: Bearing, Traveling, and Nursing. PSA speakers live in a riverine, forested region (The Orinoco vally at first then expanding west and north) where fishing, wetland animals, early pig-analog management, and household obligation are central. The language is reconstructed for the period just before a major expansion, likely connected to improved fish and pig farming, river travel, and growing inter-household ritual networks.

Two closely replated sapient species share this world. Their relationship resembles a caste-like social arrangement, though it is less directly exploitative than comparable human systems because their evolved social rewards encourage interspecies cooperation more than direct competition. The smaller A. minorus often appears uncomfortably subservient to American human observers, and this relationship may influence PSA’s deixis, address systems, obligation vocabulary, and social registers. Comunities have been mixed since predating A. minor’s develoupment of speach, so usually any variation between speices is closer to class based “dialects” or “private languages” among an extended family then true seperate languages, and even that level of difference is almost always a skew in ‘register’ use rather than a difference in capabilities. That is in a given region the linguistic varriation is larly between locations and households, not split into A. minor and M. major comunities. ‘Housholds’ are large by human standards, 5 is the minimum realistic size, and >10 is more normal. They don’t really pair bond the way we do, instead a given group is generaly fairly promiscous with sex being more about social status and comfort then either an pair bond or herem like system. This varies less then it does in humans, but this should still be considered just the standard base line, the way small extended families is the human default.

PSA speakers are short-muzzled mammals with carnivore-descended dentition, but their lips, tongue, and jaw control support broadly humanlike articulation. Their front teeth and lips permit bilabials and rounded vowels, while their non-primate tooth pattern makes labiodentals and dental fricatives rare or absent. PSA therefore favors bilabials, alveolar/postalveolar stops and affricates, velars, nasals, liquids, glides, /s h/, and glottal contrasts rather than fine dental or labiodental contrasts.

## phonotactic notes from examining the PPSA_to_PSA.lsc file

PSA strongly prefers open or simple closed syllables, with no ordinary consonant clusters. The normal surface syllable shapes are V, CV, VC, and CVC, though V and VC mostly occur word-initially or through erosion. The affricate /ts/ behaves as a single consonant, not a cluster.

Complexity is concentrated in the nucleus rather than the margins. High vowels become glides beside other vowels, producing surface /j w/ and vowel-glide sequences. Older long vowels and vowel sequences often become diphthongs or glide-vowel transitions.

Final nasals are unstable. Earlier /n/ nasalizes the preceding vowel and is then lost word-finally or before consonants, producing nasal vowels /ã ĩ ũ/. Coda consonants survive best as oral stops, /s/, /m/, /k/, /t/, and fossilized remnants of older nasal clusters.

The glottal stop /ʔ/ marks prosodic word boundaries and vowel-initial words. It is not a normal coda and is deleted or suppressed near consonants.
